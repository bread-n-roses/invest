  1. Question

    Consider stock XYZ. The share price of stock XYZ after 6 months is $46.2\$46.2.

    If you had bought one put option contract with a strike price of $50\$50 for a premium of $5.9\$5.9, what would be your holding period return over 6 months?

    State your answer as a fraction and round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 51% becomes 0.51.


    Solution

    The solution is -0.36 or -36%.

    The payoff to the put option is 100⋅max⁡($50−$46.2,0)=$380100 \cdot \max(\$50 - \$46.2,0) = \$380.

    The profit is then $380−(100⋅$5.9)=$−210\$380 - (100 \cdot \$5.9) = \$-210. Finally, the HPR is $−210100⋅5.9=−0.36\frac{\$-210}{100 \cdot 5.9} = -0.36.


  2. Question

    The price of a stock is currently $36\$36. Over the course of the next year, the price is anticipated to rise to $40\$40 or decline to $32\$32.

    The upside has a 0.62 (i.e. 62%) probability of occurring and the annualized risk-free interest rate is 3%.

    What is the price of a one-year put option with an exercise price of $35\$35 using the binomial model?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 2.69.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 1.111.11.

    The payoff is either max⁡($35−$40,0)=$0\max(\$35 - \$40, 0) = \$0 in the up state, or max⁡($35−$32,0)=$3\max(\$35 - \$32, 0) = \$3 in the down state.

    The upside probability is 0.62. The downside probability must then be 0.38.

    The value of the put option today is then P=($3⋅0.38)/1.03=$1.11P = (\$3 \cdot 0.38)/1.03 = \$1.11.

  4. Question

    Suppose the current price of a non-dividend paying stock is $91. In the next year, the stock either goes up by 5% or down by 5%.

    A one-year put option on the stock is available with an exercise price of $92.

    What is the hedge ratio of the put option? Round your answer to two decimals. Example answer: -0.37.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: −0.61-0.61.

    The hedge ratio for a put is H=P1u−P1dS1u−S1dH = \frac{P_1^u - P_1^d}{S_1^u - S_1^d}.

    The payoff of the put is max⁡(X−ST,0)\max(X - S_T, 0).

    S0=$91S_0 = \$91 such that S1u=$95.55S_1^u = \$95.55 and S1d=$86.45S_1^d = \$86.45. In this case P1u=$0P_1^u = \$0 and P1d=$5.55P_1^d= \$5.55.

    The hedge ratio becomes H=$0−$5.55$95.55−$86.45=−0.61H = \frac{\$0 - \$5.55}{\$95.55 - \$86.45} = -0.61.

  6. Question

    PAL Corporation has a current share price S0S_0 of $43\$43.

    Consider a 3-month call option on PAL Corporation stock. The strike price is $43\$43 and the call option is correctly priced at a premium of $5.70\$5.70. The annual risk-free rate is 3%.

    A 3-month put option on PAL stock trades for $3.75\$3.75, which constitutes an arbitrage opportunity. You decide to invest in (i.e. buy or sell) one put option, and set up an arbitrage strategy accordingly.

    What is your arbitrage profit? Round your answer to two decimals. For example, 2.74.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 1.631.63.

    The stock price is currently $43\$43, the strike price is $43\$43, and the call is correctly priced at a premium of $5.7\$5.7.

    Using the put-call parity, figure out what the put premium should be: $5.7+$43(1.03)(1/4)=$43+P0\$5.7 + \frac{\$43}{(1.03)^{(1/4)}} = \$43 + P_0.

    $5.7+$43(1.03)(1/4)−$43=P0→P0≈$5.383.\$5.7 + \frac{\$43}{(1.03)^{(1/4)}} - \$43 = P_0 \; \longrightarrow{} \; P_0 \approx \$5.383. The put however, is currently priced at $3.75\$3.75, which is far too low! So the put is underpriced, which means we would like to buy it. Our arbitrage profit will be equal to the difference between the put premiums: $5.383−$3.75=$1.63\$5.383 - \$3.75 = \$1.63.

    You can also show this more formally, but in this question you do not necessarily have to, as the question only asks for the profit itself, and not a derivation.

    If you are interested in the correct arbitrage strategy in this question, consider that we are buying the put because the put is underpriced. This is similar to the strategy we have seen in Tutorial 4 (and in Lecture Week 4). We buy a put, buy a stock, sell a call, and borrow money against the risk-free rate (use the put-call parity to verify this).

    At t=0t=0, we buy the put and the stock, so we obtain −$43−$3.75=$−46.75-\$43 - \$3.75 = \$-46.75. We sell a call and borrow against the risk-free rate, so we obtain $5.7+$43(1.03)(1/4)=$48.383\$5.7 + \frac{\$43}{(1.03)^{(1/4)}} = \$48.383. Adding the two, we have $−46.75+$48.383=$1.63\$-46.75 + \$48.383 = \$1.63.


  7. Question

    Suppose the current price of a non-dividend paying stock is $49\$49. In the next year, the stock either goes up by 10% or down by 10%. The risk-neutral probabilities are 0.6 and 0.4 for an upward and downward movement, respectively, and the annual risk-free rate is 2%.

    A one-year digital call option on the stock is available with a strike price of $45\$45.

    A digital call option is an option that pays a fixed amount if the call option is in-the-money, suppose in this case it pays $17\$17, and pays $0\$0 if the call option is out-of-the money.

    What is the price of the digital call option? Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 4.67.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 1010.

    The initial stock price is $49\$49. Then S1u=$49⋅1.1=$53.9S_1^u = \$49 \cdot 1.1 = \$53.9 and S1d=$49⋅0.9=$44.1S_1^d = \$49 \cdot 0.9 = \$44.1.

    Suppose the digital call option pays $17\$17 if the call is in the money.

    In the up state: max⁡($53.9−$45,0)>0\max(\$53.9 - \$45, 0) > 0 so the digital call option pays $17\$17. In the down state: max⁡($44.1−$45,0)=0\max(\$44.1 - \$45, 0) = 0 so the digital call option pays nothing.

    The price of the digital call option today is then (0.6⋅$17+0.4⋅$0)/1.02=$10(0.6 \cdot \$17 + 0.4 \cdot \$0)/1.02 = \$10.


  8. Question

    An American-style call option with three months to maturity has a strike price of $26. The underlying stock now sells for $32.6. The call premium is $14.

    What is the time value of the call? Round your answer to one decimal. For example, 6.2.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 7.47.4.

    The intrinsic value of the option is the payoff of the option if it were exercised today: max⁡(ST−X,0)\max(S_T - X,0). The time value of the option is then the part of the option value (premium) which cannot be explained by the option’s intrinsic value. Hence, time value = C−max⁡(ST−X,0)C-\max(S_T-X,0).

    The strike price is $26\$26, the current stock price is $32.6\$32.6, and the call premium is $14\$14. The time value of the option is then $14−$32.6+$26=$7.4.\$14 - \$32.6 + \$26 = \$7.4.


  9. Question

    You purchase one September 53 put contract for a put premium of $2. Remember that each stock option contract provides the right to buy/sell 100 shares of stock.

    What is the maximum profit that you could gain from this strategy?

    Report your answer as an integer without the dollar sign. For example, $6700 becomes 6700.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 51005100.

    The maximum profit is reached when STS_T drops to zero, in the most extreme case. The maximum profit is this payoff, but we have to subtract the premium that was paid for the put option.

    The strike price is $53\$53 and the premium is $2\$2. The maximum profit is then max⁡($53−$0,0)−$2=$51\max(\$53 - \$0, 0) - \$2 = \$51.

    One contract contains 100 options, so our total maximum profit is $51⋅100=$5100\$51 \cdot 100 = \$5100.


  10. Question

    Consider a non-dividend paying stock which has a current stock price of $72.5. In the next year, the stock price either goes up by 8.4% or down by 8.4%.

    The discrete, annual risk-free rate is 4%.

    What is the risk-neutral probability of the upward movement?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 0.53.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.740.74.

    The formula for the risk-neutral probability of an upward price movement is: q=(1+r)−du−dq = \frac{(1+r) - d}{u - d}.

    The stock price can go up or down by 8.4%, so u=1.084u = 1.084 and d=0.916d = 0.916.

    The risk-neutral probability is then q=1.04−0.9161.084−0.916≈0.74.q = \frac{1.04 - 0.916}{1.084 - 0.916} \approx 0.74.
