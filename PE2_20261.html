  1. Question

    Suppose that there are two independent economic factors, F1 and F2. The risk-free rate is 4%, and all stocks have independent firm-specific components with a standard deviation of 23%.

    Portfolios A and B are both well-diversified with the following properties:

    Portfolio Beta on F1 Beta on F2 Expected Return
    A 1.3 1.8 0.28
    B 2.1 -0.5 0.24

    What is the risk premium on Factor 1, i.e. RP1\text{RP}_{1}?

    Round your answer to two decimals. For example, 0.24.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.110.11.

    The following equation applies here: 𝔼(rP)=rf+βP,1(𝔼(r1)−rf)+βP,2(𝔼(r2)−rf)\mathbb{E}(r_P) = r_f + \beta_{P,1}(\mathbb{E}(r_1) - rf) + \beta_{P,2}(\mathbb{E}(r_2) - rf) for a general portfolio P (can be either A or B here).

    We need to find the risk premium (RP) for each of the two factors: RP1=(𝔼(r1)−rf)\text{RP}_{1} = (\mathbb{E}(r_1) - r_f) and RP2=(𝔼(r2)−rf)\text{RP}_{2} = (\mathbb{E}(r_2) - r_f).

    To do so, solve the following system of two equations with two unknowns: 0.28=0.04+(1.3⋅RP1)+(1.8⋅RP2)0.28 = 0.04 + (1.3 \cdot \text{RP}_1) + (1.8 \cdot \text{RP}_2) 0.24=0.04+(2.1⋅RP1)+(−0.5⋅RP2)0.24 = 0.04 + (2.1 \cdot \text{RP}_1) + (-0.5 \cdot \text{RP}_2)

    Simplifying gives: RP2=0.24−1.3RP11.8\text{RP}_2 = \frac{0.24 - 1.3 \text{RP}_1}{1.8} and RP2=0.2−2.1RP1−0.5\text{RP}_2 = \frac{0.2 - 2.1 \text{RP}_1}{-0.5}.

    Equating the two expressions yields RP1=0.108352≈0.11\text{RP}_1 = 0.108352 \approx 0.11 or 11%.


  2. Question

    The following are estimates for two stocks
    Stock Expected Return Beta Firm-Specific Standard Deviation
    A 0.12 0.9 0.31
    B 0.19 1.4 0.39

    The market index has a standard deviation of 0.22.

    Suppose that we were to construct a portfolio with proportions 30% in Stock A, 45% in Stock B, and 25% in T-bills.

    What is the total (i.e. systematic + non-systematic) standard deviation of this portfolio? Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.482.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.2810.281.

    First, calculate the beta of the portfolio. This is simply a weighted average of each beta.

    βP=wAβA+wBβB+wrfβrf→βP=0.30⋅0.9+0.45⋅1.4+0.25⋅0=0.9\beta_P = w_A \beta_A + w_B \beta_B + w_{r_f} \beta_{r_f} \longrightarrow{} \beta_P = 0.30 \cdot 0.9 + \, 0.45 \cdot 1.4 + \, 0.25 \cdot 0 = 0.9.

    The total variance of the portfolio is σP2=βP2σM2+σ2(eP)\sigma^2_P = \beta^2_P \sigma^2_M + \sigma^2(e_P) where βP2σM2\beta^2_P \sigma^2_M is the systematic risk and σ2(eP)\sigma^2(e_P) is the non-systematic risk (firm-specific variance).

    Obtain the firm-specific variance as: σ2(eP)=wA⋅σ2(eA)+wB⋅σ2(eB)+wrf2⋅σ2(erf)\sigma^2(e_P)=w^_A\cdot \sigma^2(e_A) + w^_B\cdot \sigma^2(e_B) + w^2_{r_f}\cdot \sigma^2(e_{r_f}) or (0.32⋅0.312)+(0.452⋅0.392)+(0.252⋅0)=0.03944925(0.3^2\cdot 0.31^2)+(0.45^2 \cdot 0.39^2)+(0.25^2 \cdot 0) = 0.03944925.

    The total risk (standard deviation) of the portfolio is then σP=σP2=βP2σM2+σ2(eP)\sigma_P = \sqrt{\sigma^2_P} = \sqrt{ \beta^2_P \sigma^2_M + \sigma^2(e_P)}, so σP=(0.92⋅0.222)+0.03944925=0.078653≈0.281\sigma_P = \sqrt{(0.9^2 \cdot 0.22^2) + 0.03944925} = \sqrt{0.078653} \approx 0.281.


  3. Question

    Boeing has a beta of 1.2. The annualized market return yesterday was 10%, and the risk-free rate is currently 5%.

    Yesterday, Boeing announced that it will take over another airplane manufacturer and when trade was closing, Boeing’s annualized return was 16%.

    Using the market model, what is yesterday’s abnormal return of Boeing?

    Report your answer as a fraction and round your answer to three decimals. If your answer is 4.21%, it is reported as 0.042.


    Solution

    Market model = CAPM: AR=rb−(rf+β(rm−rf))AR = r_b - (r_f + \beta(r_m - r_f)).

    Filling in what we know, we obtain AR=0.16−(0.05+1.2(0.1−0.05))=0.05AR = 0.16 - (0.05 + 1.2(0.1 - 0.05)) = 0.05.


  4. Question

    Suppose that we live in a one-factor economy.

    We have the following information about two well-diversified and well-priced portfolios in this economy.

    Expected return Beta
    Portfolio A 0.16 1.2
    Portfolio B 0.08 0.0

    A third well-diversified portfolio emerges, portfolio C, which has a beta of 0.6 and an expected return of 0.06.

    Portfolio C turns out to be mispriced. To take advantage of this arbitrage opportunity, we construct an equally-weighted portfolio of A and B. What would be our arbitrage return?

    Round your answer to three decimal places. For example, 0.036.


    Solution

    If we construct an equally-weighted portfolio using Portfolio A and B, our exposure to the single risk factor is 0.6, which is exactly the same exposure that Portfolio C has to the single risk factor.

    The expected return of the arbitrage portfolio is: 0.5⋅ra+0.5⋅rB0.5 \cdot r_a + 0.5 \cdot r_B.

    The expected return from combining A and B is 0.5⋅0.16+0.5⋅0.08=0.120.5 \cdot 0.16 + 0.5 \cdot 0.08 = 0.12. This is higher than the expected return on portfolio C, which is 0.06. Therefore, our arbitrage strategy is to buy C (underpriced) and sell A+B, to lock in an arbitrage profit of 0.12−0.06=0.060.12 - 0.06 = 0.06.

  7. Question

    Suppose we want to conduct an event study. Consider two stocks, i={1,2}i = \{1, 2\}.

    You calculate that stock 1 and stock 2 have abnormal returns on the event day of 4.0% and 6.1%, respectively.

    We would like to know whether the abnormal returns on the event day are significant for the average firm. The average firm is an equal-weighted combination of stock 1 and 2.

    We are given that the variance of the equal-weighted abnormal returns during the 200-day estimation window is σ2(ARtew)=9.9%\sigma^2(AR^{\text{ew}}_t) = 9.9\%.

    Report the t-value of the abnormal returns of the average firm on the day of the event.

    Round your answer to three decimals. Example answer: 0.592.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.160.16.

    The abnormal return of the average firm is a weighted combination of the abnormal returns of Stock 1 and Stock 2.

    ARew=∑i=12wiARi=0.5⋅4.0%+0.5⋅6.1%=5.05%.AR^{ew} = \sum^2_{i=1} w_i AR_i = 0.5 \cdot 4.0\% + 0.5 \cdot 6.1\% = 5.05\%.

    To obtain the t-value, we must divide by the standard deviation of the equal-weighted abnormal returns during the 200-day estimation window. Tew=ARewσAR⋅1+1T=0.05050.31464⋅1+1200=0.05050.3154=≈0.16T^{ew} = \frac{AR^{ew}}{\sigma_{AR}\cdot\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{T}}} = \frac{0.0505}{0.31464\cdot\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{200}}} = \frac{0.0505}{0.3154} = \approx 0.16.

    See Lecture Week 2 Slide ‘Univariate Analysis for statistical significance’.


  8. Question

    Consider the CAPM. The market’s expected return is 0.11 and the risk-free rate is 0.04.

    Stock 1 has a return of 0.112 and systematic risk of β1=0.9\beta_1 = 0.9, and Stock 2 has a return of 0.136 and systematic risk of β2=1.4\beta_2 = 1.4.

    Report the alpha of the stock that a rational investor would pursue.

    Round your answer to three decimal places. Example answer: 0.028.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.0090.009.

    Remember the CAPM equation for alpha: αi=ri−rf−βi(rm−rf)\alpha_i = r_i - r_f - \beta_i(r_m - r_f).

    The alpha of stock A is 0.112−0.04−0.9*(0.11−0.04)=0.0090.112 - 0.04 - 0.9 * (0.11 - 0.04) = 0.009 and the alpha of stock B is 0.136−0.04−1.4*(0.11−0.04)=−0.0020.136 - 0.04 - 1.4 * (0.11 - 0.04) = -0.002.

    A rational investor would choose the stock with the highest alpha, so the correct answer is 0.0090.009.
