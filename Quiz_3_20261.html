  1. Question

    Consider the two (excess return) index-model regressions for stocks AA and BB. The risk-free rate over the period was 0.03, and the market’s average return was 0.12. The estimations from the index-model regression are:

      Stock A Stock B
    Index model regression estimates 0.01 + 1.5(rm−rf)(r_m - r_f) 0.02 + 1.7(rm−rf)(r_m - r_f)
    R-square 0.623 0.461
    Residual standard deviation 0.111 0.199
    Standard deviation of excess returns 0.224 0.265

    Report the Treynor measure of the best performing stock.

    Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.085.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.1020.102.

    The Treynor measure (TM) is obtained as: ri−rfβi\frac{r_i - r_f}{\beta_i}, so we first need to obtain the excess expected return from the index model regression: ri−rf=αi+βi(rm−rf)r_i - r_f = \alpha_i + \beta_i (r_m - r_f).

    For Stock A: The excess return is 0.01+1.5(0.12−0.03)=0.145.0.01 + 1.5(0.12 - 0.03) = 0.145. Then the TM is 0.1451.5≈0.097\frac{0.145}{1.5} \approx 0.097.

    For Stock B: The excess return is 0.02+1.7(0.12−0.03)=0.173.0.02 + 1.7(0.12 - 0.03) = 0.173. Then the TM is 0.1731.7≈0.102\frac{0.173}{1.7} \approx 0.102.

    The TM of the best performing stock is 0.102.


  2. Question

    Consider 10 portfolios sorted by beta. The lowest-beta portfolio has a beta of 0.2. The highest-beta portfolio has a beta of 2.4.

    The Security Market Line implies that for every 0.2 increase in beta, the expected return increases by 0.005 (0.5%). Assume that the risk-free rate is 0.

    What is the expected return difference between the highest- and lowest-beta portfolios according to the Security Market Line (SML)?

    Round your answer to three decimal places. Example answer: 0.091.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.0550.055.

    The SML implies that the expected return increases by 0.005 per 0.2 beta increase, so:

    Slope of the SML = 0.005/0.2=0.0250.005/0.2 = 0.025 per unit of beta

    Beta difference = 2.4 - 0.2 = 2.2.

    Expected return difference = 2.2⋅0.025=0.0552.2 \cdot 0.025 = 0.055.


  3. Question

    Consider a universe of stocks that all have a beta of 1.8 on the market index, which is the only risk factor in the economy.

    An analyst finds 5 stocks with alphas of 0.022, and 10 stocks with alphas of -0.017. Suppose the analyst decides to invest $10,000 in each of the 5 positive alpha stocks, and short an amount of $5,000 in each of the 10 negative alpha stocks.

    What is the profit (in dollars) of the analyst’s strategy?

    Report your answer without the dollar sign. For example, $22,500 becomes 22500.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 19501950.

    The analyst’s exposure to the positive alpha stocks is 5⋅$10,000=$50,0005 \cdot \$10,000 = \$50,000. Similarly, the exposure to the negative stocks is 10⋅$5,000=$50,00010 \cdot \$5,000 = \$50,000.

    The profit on a portfolio in which you buy positive alpha stocks and go short in negative alpha stocks is: 𝔼[rp]⋅$exposure=[rf+0.022+1.8(rm−rf)]⋅$50,000−[rf−0.017+1.8(rm−rf)]⋅$50,000\mathbb{E}[r_p] \cdot \$\,\text{exposure} = \big[r_f + 0.022 + 1.8 (r_m - r_f) \big] \cdot \$50,000 - \big[r_f - 0.017 + 1.8 (r_m - r_f) \big] \cdot \$50,000. This then simplifies to: [0.022+0.017]⋅$50,000=$1950[0.022 + 0.017] \cdot \$50,000 = \$1950.


  4. Question

    You are constructing a market-neutral portfolio using the following assets:

    • Stock A: Long investment of $1,000 with a beta of 0.79.
    • Stock B: Long investment of $500 with a beta of 1.69.
    • Stock C: Short position of $___ with a beta of 1.14.

    How much should you short in Stock C to make the portfolio market-neutral?

    Round your answer to the nearest dollar and report it as a positive value. For example, a value of -$1825.10 is reported as 1825.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 14341434.

    To neutralize market exposure, the total portfolio beta must equal zero:

    Net Beta=0.79⋅1000+1.69⋅500−1.14⋅x=0\text{Net Beta} = 0.79 \cdot 1000 + 1.69 \cdot 500 - 1.14 \cdot x = 0.

    Solving for xx:

    x=0.79⋅1000+1.69⋅5001.14=1434x = \frac{0.79 \cdot 1000 + 1.69 \cdot 500}{1.14} = 1434.

  6. Question

    The following data are available relating to the performance of Fund ABC and the market portfolio:

      Fund ABC Market Portfolio
    Average return 0.23 0.16
    Standard deviations of returns 0.32 0.13
    Beta 1.6 1.0
    Residual standard deviation 0.08 0

     

    The risk-free return during the sample period was 0.04.

    If you wanted to evaluate Fund ABC using the M2M^2 measure (with the market portfolio as the benchmark portfolio), what percent of the adjusted portfolio would need to be invested in T-Bills?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 0.84.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.590.59.

    Recall that we need to adjust the return of Fund ABC for the difference in risk between the fund and the market portfolio.

    The difference is risk is σmσABC\frac{\sigma_m}{\sigma_{\text{ABC}}}.

    The risk of Fund ABC is 0.32 and the market risk is 0.13. In this case the ratio becomes: 0.13/0.32≈0.410.13/0.32 \approx 0.41.

    Hence, to obtain an adjusted portfolio with risk of 0.13 (comparable to the market), we would need to invest 41% in fund ABC, which has a standard deviation of 0.32, and 5959% in the risk-free asset, which has no risk.


  7. Question

    Assume that stock market returns have the market index as a common factor and that every stock in the economy has a beta of 1.7 on the market index.

    Firm-specific risk (standard deviation) is independent across firms and is estimated at 0.27 for each of the firms.

    Suppose that an analyst studies 16 stocks and finds that half of them have an alpha of +0.01, and the other half have an alpha of -0.01.

    The analyst invests $500\$500 in an equally weighted portfolio of the positive alpha stocks, and shorts $500\$500 of an equally weighted portfolio of the negative alpha stocks.

    What is the total risk (standard deviation) of the analyst’s profit?

    Round your answer to the nearest dollar. Example: 25.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 6868.

    We have N=16N = 16 stocks in total, of which N/2=8N/2 = 8 are positive alpha stocks and N/2=$8N/2 = \$8 are negative alpha stocks. This means we invest in $ investmentN/2=$500/8=$62.5\frac{\$\text{ investment}}{N/2} = \$500/8 = \$62.5 in each of the 16 stocks.

    Due to our symmetry assumptions, the variance of the profit is: σp−n2=N⋅(($ investmentN/2⋅σε)2)\sigma_{p-n}^2 = N \cdot ((\frac{\$\text{ investment}}{N/2} \cdot \sigma_{\varepsilon})^2).

    Filling in what we know, we obtain: σp−n2=16⋅((62.5⋅0.27)2)=$4556.25\sigma_{p-n}^2 = 16 \cdot ((62.5 \cdot 0.27)^2) = \$4556.25.

    The risk (in terms of standard deviation) is then: σp−n=$4556.25=$68\sigma_{p-n} = \sqrt{\$4556.25} = \$68.


  8. Question

    Consider stock 1 and stock 2. Suppose you have estimated the Fama-French 3-factor model for stock 1 and stock 2 and obtain the following results.

    Stock 1: r1−rf=0.01+0.8(rM−rf)+0.9SMB+2.1HMLr_1 - r_f = 0.01 + 0.8\,(r_M - r_f) + 0.9 \, \text{SMB} + 2.1 \, \text{HML}

    You would like to create a portfolio of the two stocks which has a loading of 1.3 on the HML factor. If you need to be fully invested, how much should you invest in stock 1 to achieve this?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. Example answer: 0.53.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.470.47.

    The HML loading of Stock 1 is 2.1 and that of Stock 2 is 0.6.

    If our target loading on the HML factor is 1.3, we then need to solve for the following equation: 1.3=2.1w1+0.6w2→1.3=2.1w1+0.6(1−w1)→1.3=2.1w1+0.6−0.6w11.3 = 2.1 w_1 + 0.6 w_2 \longrightarrow{} 1.3 = 2.1 w_1 + 0.6 (1-w_1) \longrightarrow{} 1.3 = 2.1w_1 + 0.6 - 0.6 w_1.

    Solving for w1w_1 gives w1=1.3−0.62.1−0.6≈0.47w_1 = \frac{1.3 - 0.6}{2.1 - 0.6} \approx 0.47.


  9. Question

    Nicholas Manufacturing just announced yesterday that its fourth quarter earnings will be 10% higher than last year’s fourth quarter.

    Nicholas Manufacturing had an abnormal (i.e., unexpected) return of 0.018 yesterday. Today, the market value of its outstanding equity is $160 million.

    What was the market capitalization of Nicholas Manufacturing before the positive earnings announcement?

    Round your answer to millions and report your answer without the dollar sign. For example, if you find a market capitalization of $158,700,000\$158,700,000 or $158.7\$158.7 million, report your answer as 159.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 157157.

    MCnew=(1+abnormal return)⋅MColdMC_{\text{new}} = (1 + \text{abnormal return}) \cdot MC_{\text{old}} or similarly MCnew/(1+abnormal return)=MColdMC_{\text{new}}/ (1 + \text{abnormal return}) = MC_{\text{old}}.

    If the abnormal return was 0.018 and the current market capitalization is $160\$ 160 million, then the market capitalization before the positive earnings announcement is $160/(1+0.018)≈$157.1709234≈$157\$160/(1+0.018) \approx \$157.1709234 \approx \$157 (in millions).


  10. Question

    Suppose the market portfolio has a Sharpe ratio of 0.64 and a volatility of 0.16. Fund A has a Sharpe ratio of 0.52.

    Report the M2M^2 for Fund A. Round your answer to three decimal places. For example, -0.031.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: −0.019-0.019.

    M2=σm(SRA−SRm)M^2 = \sigma_m (SR_A - SR_m).

    M2=0.16(0.52−0.64)=−0.019M^2 = 0.16 (0.52 - 0.64) = -0.019.
