  1. Question

    Consider stock B. The historical relationship between returns on this stock and the market portfolio has been estimated from an index model regression as: rB=−0.002+0.8rMr_B = -0.002 + 0.8r_M. All returns are excess returns.

    Today, positive news was released about stock B. This positive news was reflected in the stock price as stock B outperformed the prediction of the index model.

    Specifically, today’s rate of return on stock B was rBr_B = 0.038. The market today responded to very encouraging news about the unemployment rate, and today’s market rate of return was rMr_M = 0.03.

    By how much did Firm B outperform the prediction from the index model?

    Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.023.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.0160.016.

    The outperformance is given by the difference between the actual return and the predicted return based on the index model regression.

    Hence, 0.038−(−0.002+0.8⋅0.03)=0.0160.038 - (-0.002 + 0.8 \cdot 0.03) = 0.016. In that case Stock B outperformed by 1.61.6%-point compared to the prediction from the index model.


  2. Question

    Consider a multifactor model with two independent economic factors: F1F_1 and F2F_2.

    Suppose we have two well-diversified portfolios, A and B, that have some exposure to economic Factor 1 and Factor 2.

    Specifically, the exposures are:
    Portfolio on on
    A 0.73 0.23
    B 0.46 0.75

    Construct a new portfolio, AB, that consists of A and B and has exposure of 0.55 to F2F_2.

    What is the exposure of this portfolio to F1F_1?

    Round your answer to two decimals. For example, 0.58.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.560.56.

    First obtain the weight to be invested in Portfolio A and B, based on the exposure restriction. We have that wAβA,F2+wBβB,F2=0.55w_A \beta_{A,F_2} + w_B \beta_{B,F_2} = 0.55.

    Using the fact that the weights need to sum to one, we solve for the weight to be invested in Portfolio A: wA=0.55−βB,F2βA,F2−βB,F2=0.55−0.750.23−0.75=0.385w_A= \frac{0.55 - \beta_{B,F_2}}{ \beta_{A,F_2} - \beta_{B,F_2} } = \frac{0.55 - 0.75}{0.23 - 0.75} = 0.385.

    We then construct portfolio AB’s exposure to F1F_1 as βAB,F1=wAβA,F1+(1−wA)βB,F1=0.385⋅0.73+(1−0.385)0.46=0.56\beta_{AB, F_1} = w_A \beta_{A, F_1} + (1 - w_A) \beta_{B, F_1} = 0.385 \cdot 0.73 + (1 - 0.385) 0.46 = 0.56.


  3. Question

    Consider two stocks, A and B, with a beta of 0.8 and 1.3, respectively. The systematic risk (standard deviation) of the market is 0.28.

    What is the covariance between stock A and B?

    Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.021.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.0820.082.

    Recall from Lecture Week 2 that ℂov(rA,rB)=βA⋅βB⋅σm2\mathbb{C}\text{ov}(r_A, r_B) = \beta_A \cdot \beta_B \cdot \sigma_m^2.

    The covariance between stocks A and B is ℂov(rA,rB)=0.8⋅1.3⋅(0.28)2=0.082\mathbb{C}\text{ov}(r_A, r_B) = 0.8\cdot 1.3\cdot (0.28)^2= 0.082.

  5. Question

    Investment Management Inc. (IMI) uses the capital market line to make asset allocation recommendations. IMI derives the following forecasts:

    • Expected total return on the market portfolio: 0.13.
    • Standard deviation on the market portfolio: 0.26.
    • Risk-free rate: 0.02.

    Lisa seeks IMI’s advice for a portfolio asset allocation. Lisa wants the standard deviation of the portfolio to equal half that of the market portfolio.

    Using the capital market line, what expected return can IMI provide when taking Lisa’s risk constraint into account?

    Round your answer to two decimals. For example, 0.03.


    Solution

    The intercept of the CML equals rf=0.02r_f = 0.02 and the slope of the CML equals the Sharpe ratio for the market portfolio: (0.13 - 0.02)/0.26 = 0.423.

    The target standard deviation is 0.26/20.26/2.

    Therefore, using the CML: 0.02+0.26/2⋅0.423=0.070.02 + 0.26/2 \cdot 0.423 = 0.07.


  6. Question

    Consider an event study. In the 150 days prior to the event, you have estimated the SIM for stock A and find the following estimates: αÂ=0.003\hat{\alpha_A} = 0.003 and β̂A=1.5\hat{\beta}_A = 1.5.

    On the day of the event the return on stock A is 0.179. Furthermore, the risk-free rate is 0.03 and the return of the market is 0.13.

    Using the market model, what is the abnormal return on the day of the event?

    Round your answer to three decimal places. For example, 0.042.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: −0.004-0.004.

    The abnormal return on the day of the event is: ARt=rA−0.03−(α+βA⋅(0.13−0.03))AR_t=r_A-0.03-(\alpha+\beta_A\cdot(0.13-0.03)).

    This becomes: ARt=0.179−0.03−(0.003+1.5⋅(0.13−0.03))=−0.004AR_t=0.179 - 0.03 - (0.003+1.5\cdot(0.13-0.03)) = -0.004.

  9. Question

    Consider the Fama-French 3-Factor model: ri=αi+βirm+γiSMB+ϕiHML+εir_{i} = \alpha_i + \beta_i r_m + \gamma_i \text{SMB} + \phi_i \text{HML} + \varepsilon_i, where all returns are in excess of the risk-free rate.

    Suppose you find the following estimates for Stock ABC: αABC=−0.002\alpha_{ABC} = - 0.002, βABC=1.2\beta_{ABC} = 1.2, γABC=−0.51\gamma_{ABC} = -0.51, and ϕABC=0.35\phi_{ABC} = 0.35.

    Moreover, it is given that the variance of the market portfolio, the variance of the SMB portfolio, and the variance of the HML portfolio are σm2=0.05\sigma^2_m = 0.05, σSMB2=0.06\sigma^2_{\text{SMB}} = 0.06,and σHML2=0.05\sigma^2_{\text{HML}} = 0.05, respectively. You can assume that the systematic risk factors are uncorrelated.

    If Stock ABC has a total variance of 0.25, what is the non-systematic risk (= firm-specific variance) of Stock ABC?

    Round your answer to three decimal places. Example answer: 0.091.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.1560.156.

    The systematic risk comes from the three risk factors: the market, the SMB portfolio, and the HML portfolio.

    The systematic risk therefore is β12σm2+γ12σSMB2+ϕ12σHML2\beta^2_1 \sigma^2_{m} + \gamma^2_1 \sigma^2_{\text{SMB}} + \phi^2_1 \sigma^2_{\text{HML}}.

    The non-systematic risk is the total variance minus the systematic risk, so σε2=σABC2−(β12σm2+γ12σSMB2+ϕ12σHML2)\sigma^2_{\varepsilon} = \sigma^2_{\text{ABC}} - (\beta^2_1 \sigma^2_{m} + \gamma^2_1 \sigma^2_{\text{SMB}} + \phi^2_1 \sigma^2_{\text{HML}}).

    Filling in the given values, yields: σε2=0.25−(1.22⋅0.05+(−0.51)2⋅0.06+(0.35)2⋅0.05)=0.156\sigma^2_{\varepsilon} = 0.25 - (1.2^2 \cdot 0.05 + (-0.51)^2 \cdot 0.06 + (0.35)^2 \cdot 0.05) = 0.156.

    Solution
