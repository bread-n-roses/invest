  2. Question

    We want to evaluate the performance of mutual fund A and mutual fund B using the information ratio measure for performance evaluation.

    The risk-free rate during the sample period is 0.03, and the average return on the market portfolio is 0.15.

    Mutual fund A has an average return of 0.12, a residual standard deviation of 0.025, and a beta of 1.5. Mutual fund B has an average return of 0.2, a residual standard deviation of 0.026, and a beta of 0.7.

    Report the information ratio of the best-performing mutual fund. Round your answer to two decimals. Example answer: 2.59.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 3.313.31.

    The information ratio is the alpha of a portfolio divided by its non-systematic risk: IR=ασεIR = \frac{\alpha}{\sigma_{\varepsilon}}.

    Mutual fund A has an information ratio of: IRA=(0.12−0.03−1.5(0.15−0.03))/0.025≈−3.6IR_A = (0.12 - 0.03 - 1.5(0.15-0.03))/0.025 \approx -3.6.

    Mutual fund B has an information ratio of: IRB=(0.2−0.03−0.7(0.15−0.03))/0.026≈3.31IR_B = (0.2 - 0.03 - 0.7(0.15-0.03))/0.026 \approx 3.31.

    The highest information ratio is 3.313.31.


  3. Question

    Using linear regression, you estimate the CAPM based on 500 daily observations on stock returns, market returns, and risk-free rates.

    Suppose you obtain the following relationship for Stock 1: r1−rf=0.0012+1.3(rm−rf)r_1 - r_f = 0.0012 + 1.3 (r_m - r_f). The standard deviation of your alpha estimate is 0.02.

    You would like to know whether alpha is significantly different from zero at a 5% significance level.

    Report the t-value of this test. Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 3.08.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 1.341.34.

    H0:α=0H_0: \alpha = 0 vs. H1:α≠0H_1: \alpha \neq 0.

    The t-value is obtained as: T=α−αH0σ/NT = \frac{\alpha - \alpha_{H_0}}{\sigma/\sqrt{N}} where αH0\alpha_{H_0} is the alpha if the null hypothesis were true, which in this case is zero, and N is the number of observations.

    We calculate the t-value as T=0.00120.02/500≈1.34.T = \frac{0.0012}{0.02 / \sqrt{500}} \approx 1.34.


  4. Question

    Consider a universe of stocks that all have a beta of 1.8 on the market index, which is the only risk factor in the economy.

    An analyst finds 5 stocks with alphas of 0.023, and 10 stocks with alphas of -0.019. Suppose the analyst decides to invest $10,000 in each of the 5 positive alpha stocks, and short an amount of $5,000 in each of the 10 negative alpha stocks.

    What is the profit (in dollars) of the analyst’s strategy?

    Report your answer without the dollar sign. For example, $22,500 becomes 22500.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 21002100.

    The analyst’s exposure to the positive alpha stocks is 5⋅$10,000=$50,0005 \cdot \$10,000 = \$50,000. Similarly, the exposure to the negative stocks is 10⋅$5,000=$50,00010 \cdot \$5,000 = \$50,000.

    The profit on a portfolio in which you buy positive alpha stocks and go short in negative alpha stocks is: 𝔼[rp]⋅$exposure=[rf+0.023+1.8(rm−rf)]⋅$50,000−[rf−0.019+1.8(rm−rf)]⋅$50,000\mathbb{E}[r_p] \cdot \$\,\text{exposure} = \big[r_f + 0.023 + 1.8 (r_m - r_f) \big] \cdot \$50,000 - \big[r_f - 0.019 + 1.8 (r_m - r_f) \big] \cdot \$50,000. This then simplifies to: [0.023+0.019]⋅$50,000=$2100[0.023 + 0.019] \cdot \$50,000 = \$2100.

  6. Question

    Fama and French break the U.S. stock population first into three groups based on B/M ratios: the bottom 30% (Low), the middle 40% (Medium), and the top 30% (High). Then, six portfolios are created based on the intersections of the size and B/M sorts: Small/Low, Small/Medium, Small/High, Big/Low, Big/Medium, and Big/High. 

    The following returns on the six portfolios are given:

    0.09
    0.15
    0.19
    0.06
    0.11
    0.14

    The returns on a value portfolio are calculated as 12(RS/H+RB/H)\frac{1}{2}\Big(R_{S/H} + R_{B/H} \Big).

    The returns on a growth portfolio are calculated as 12(RS/L+RB/L)\frac{1}{2}\Big(R_{S/L} + R_{B/L} \Big).

    What is the return on the zero-net investment factor HML (High Minus Low)?

    Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.029.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.090.09.

    The return on the HML portfolio is RHML=RH−RLR_{HML} = R_H - R_L = value minus growth. See also Ch. 13.3.

    Hence, RHML=12(RS/H+RB/H)−12(RS/L+RB/L)R_{HML} = \frac{1}{2}\Big(R_{S/H} + R_{B/H} \Big) - \frac{1}{2}\Big(R_{S/L} + R_{B/L} \Big).

    The return on the HML portfolio is 12(0.19+0.14)−12(0.09+0.06)≈0.09\frac{1}{2}(0.19 + 0.14) - \frac{1}{2}(0.09 + 0.06) \approx 0.09.


  7. Question

    Consider the two (excess return) index-model regressions for stocks AA and BB. The risk-free rate over the period was 0.03, and the market’s average return was 0.12. The estimations from the index-model regression are:

      Stock A Stock B
    Index model regression estimates 0.01 + 1.1(rm−rf)(r_m - r_f) 0.02 + 2.2(rm−rf)(r_m - r_f)
    R-square 0.623 0.461
    Residual standard deviation 0.111 0.199
    Standard deviation of excess returns 0.224 0.265

    Report the Treynor measure of the best performing stock.

    Round your answer to three decimals. For example, 0.085.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.0990.099.

    The Treynor measure (TM) is obtained as: ri−rfβi\frac{r_i - r_f}{\beta_i}, so we first need to obtain the excess expected return from the index model regression: ri−rf=αi+βi(rm−rf)r_i - r_f = \alpha_i + \beta_i (r_m - r_f).

    For Stock A: The excess return is 0.01+1.1(0.12−0.03)=0.109.0.01 + 1.1(0.12 - 0.03) = 0.109. Then the TM is 0.1091.1≈0.099\frac{0.109}{1.1} \approx 0.099.

    For Stock B: The excess return is 0.02+2.2(0.12−0.03)=0.218.0.02 + 2.2(0.12 - 0.03) = 0.218. Then the TM is 0.2182.2≈0.099\frac{0.218}{2.2} \approx 0.099.

    The TM of the best performing stock is 0.099.


  8. Question

    The following data are available relating to the performance of Fund ABC and the market portfolio:

    Fund ABC Market Portfolio
    Average return 0.23 0.16
    Standard deviation of returns 0.27 0.14
    Beta 1 1

    The risk-free return during the sample period was 0.04.

    If you wanted to build a portfolio with Fund ABC and T-bills that has the same systemic risk as the market, which share would you need to invest in T-bills?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. Example answer: 0.53.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 00.

    Recall that the exposure is linear, such that we can write: βm=wABC⋅βABC+(1−wABC)⋅βrf\beta_m = w_{ABC} \cdot \beta_{ABC} + (1-w_{ABC}) \cdot \beta_{r_f}.

    Filling in what we know gives: 1=wABC⋅1+(1−wABC)⋅01 = w_{ABC} \cdot 1 + (1-w_{ABC}) \cdot 0. Solving for the weight gives wABC=11w_{ABC} = \frac{1}{1}. Then the weight that needs to be invested in T-bills is 1−11=1−1=01 - \frac{1}{1} = 1 - 1 = 0.
