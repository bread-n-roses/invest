  1. Question

    An American-style call option with six months to maturity has a strike price of $40.1\$40.1. The underlying stock now sells for $53.5\$53.5. The call premium is $13\$13.

    What is the intrinsic value of the call?

    Report your answer to one decimal place. For example, $13.68\$13.68 becomes 13.7.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 13.413.4.

    The intrinsic value of an option is the value the option would have if it would be exercised today.

    The value of the option today is simply the payoff to the option: max⁡(ST−X,0)\max(S_T - X, 0), which is max⁡($53.5−$40.1,0)=$13.4\max(\$53.5 - \$40.1,0) = \$13.4.

  6. Question

    You buy a one-year call option on Stock XYZ with a strike price of $84\$84. The call premium is $6.68\$6.68.

    Ignoring transaction costs, what must the share price of Stock XYZ be after one year for you to break-even on this position?

    Round your answer to two decimal places. For example, 25.72.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 90.6890.68.

    The profit/loss from the call is: max⁡(ST−X,0)−C0\max(S_T - X, 0) - C_0.

    If we break-even, it means we reach a profit/loss of zero. We equate max⁡(ST−X,0)=C0\max(S_T - X, 0) = C_0.

    As C0>0C_0 > 0 (a price is always positive), STS_T must be bigger than XX for us to break even.

    The strike price is $84\$84 and the call premium is $6.68\$6.68, so the break-even share price is ST=X+C0=$84+$6.68=$90.68.S_T = X + C_0 = \$84 + \$6.68= \$90.68.


  7. Question

    Consider Stock ABC. Two options exist: a one-year put option on Stock ABC with a strike of $47 and a premium of $2.89, and a one-year call option on Stock ABC with a strike of $47 and a premium of $5.39.

    The share price of Stock ABC is currently $47 and you believe that the share price will not fluctuate much in the coming year, so you invest in a short straddle.

    Now suppose the share price has completely dropped to zero at the end of one year (i.e. ABC is bankrupt). Given that you invested in a short straddle, what is your loss?

    Report a profit as a positive number and a negative profit (=loss) as a negative number. Round your answer to 2 decimals. For example, -36.71.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: −38.72-38.72.

    The profit/loss from the short straddle is: −max⁡(ST−X,0)−max⁡(X−ST,0)+P0+C0-\max(S_T - X, 0) - \max(X - S_T, 0) + P_0 + C_0.

    The current stock price and the strike price are $47\$47, the call premium is $5.39\$5.39, and the put premium is $2.89\$2.89.

    The profit/loss is then: −max⁡($0−$47,0)−max⁡($47−$0,0)+$2.89+$5.39-\max(\$0 - \$47, 0) - \max(\$47 - \$0, 0) + \$2.89 + \$5.39, which becomes $0−$47+$2.89+$5.39=$−38.72\$0 - \$47 + \$2.89 + \$5.39 = \$-38.72.

  9. Question

    You purchased a call option for $3.8\$3.8 17 days ago. The call has a strike price of $46\$46, and the stock is now trading for $51\$51.

    If you exercise the call today, what will be your holding-period return?

    Report your answer as a fraction and round to two decimals. For example, 0.58.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 0.320.32.

    The strike price is $46\$46, the stock is trading for $51\$51, and the call option premium is $3.8\$3.8.

    If the call is exercised, the gross profit is $51−$46=$5\$51 − \$46 = \$5. The net profit is $5−$3.8=$1.2\$5 − \$3.8 = \$1.2.

    The holding period return is then $1.2÷$3.8=0.32(32%)\$1.2 ÷ \$3.8 = 0.32 (32\%).


  10. Question

    Consider a non-dividend paying stock XYZ which has a current price of $63\$63. The premium on a one-year call option on this stock is $7\$7 and the one-year put premium is $3\$3. Suppose the annual risk-free rate is 2%.

    What must the strike price be if the put-call parity holds?

    Round your answer to one decimal place. For example, 48.3.


    Solution

    The correct answer is: 60.260.2.

    The put-call parity states that C0+X(1+rf)T=S0+P0C_0 + \frac{X}{(1+r_f)^T} = S_0 + P_0.

    The call premium is $7\$7, the put premium is $3\$3, and the current stock price is $63\$63, so we have: $7+X(1.02)=$63+$3\$7 + \frac{X}{(1.02)} = \$63 + \$3. The strike price XX must then be $60.2\$60.2.
